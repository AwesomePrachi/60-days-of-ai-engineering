# Day 23 – Startup Product Management with Claude

## Objective

Today's challenge focused on understanding customers before building products. Instead of assuming what users need, the goal was to identify the right customer, validate the core problem, and design a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) that can be tested with real users.

---

## Project

**TrustHire – Customer & MVP Blueprint**

TrustHire is an AI-powered skill-based hiring platform where candidates complete practical assessments and receive AI-generated Skill Reports, helping companies evaluate talent based on real skills rather than resumes.

---

## What I Built

Using Claude, I generated a comprehensive **Customer & MVP Blueprint** containing:

* Executive Summary
* Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
* Buyer Personas
* Top Customer Pain Points
* Customer Journey
* Buying Triggers & Customer Objections
* MVP Recommendation
* MoSCoW Feature Prioritization
* Pricing Hypothesis
* Top Risks
* 30-Day MVP Roadmap
* Founder Action Sheet
* Startup Readiness Scores
* Final Startup Verdict

---

## Biggest Learning

The biggest takeaway from today's challenge was:

> **Don't build the complete product first. Build the smallest version that solves one important customer problem and validate it before expanding.**

This mindset reduces risk, saves development time, and increases the chances of building something customers genuinely want.

---

## MVP Recommendation

The report suggested focusing on one complete workflow:

1. Candidate completes a practical challenge.
2. AI evaluates the submission.
3. AI generates a Skill Report.
4. Company reviews the report.

Only after validating this flow with real users should additional features be developed.

## Key Takeaways

* Customer problems should guide product development.
* Validate assumptions before scaling.
* Prioritize essential features using the MoSCoW framework.
* Build fast, test early, and improve based on user feedback.
* A focused MVP is more valuable than a feature-rich product.
